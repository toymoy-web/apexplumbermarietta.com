Sump Pump Installation & Repair in Marietta, GA

A sump pump is the last line of defense against a flooded basement or crawlspace, and one that fails silently is worse than not having one at all. We install and repair sump pump systems for Marietta homes, and can pair the work with waterproofing when the situation calls for it.

Warning Signs

Signs you need Sump Pump Installation in Marietta

A pump that runs constantly

Can indicate a stuck float switch, a pump that's undersized for the water volume, or a check valve issue letting water flow back down.

No sound or activity during heavy rain

The most concerning sign, a pump that should be running but isn't, often discovered only after water has already accumulated.

Unusual grinding or rattling noises

Often a sign of debris in the pump or a failing motor bearing.

A pump that's more than 7 to 10 years old and untested

Most pumps have a limited service life, and age alone is a reasonable prompt to test or replace rather than wait for a failure.

Local Cause

Why this happens in Marietta

Georgia's humid summers and occasional heavy rain events put real demand on a sump pump system, and a pump that's gone untested for a year can fail exactly when a storm finally puts it to work. Homes with finished basements in Marietta's older neighborhoods have the most to lose from a pump failure during a heavy rain event, since finished space means carpet, drywall, and stored belongings are all at risk, not just an empty concrete floor.

Diagnosis

How we find the actual problem

We test the pump under simulated conditions, pouring water into the sump pit to confirm the float switch activates and the pump actually moves water out through the discharge line, rather than just confirming the motor runs.

What This Means

The Pump You Never Think About Until It Fails

Most sump pump calls come in one of two ways: either the pump just failed during a storm and water is already accumulating, or a homeowner is being proactive after realizing they haven't checked it in years. The second scenario is obviously the better one to be in, and a simple annual test is what separates the two.

Common Questions

Sump Pump Installation FAQ

How often should a sump pump be tested?

At least once a year, ideally before the rainy season. A simple test, pouring water into the pit to confirm it activates and drains properly, takes only a few minutes.

Do I need a battery backup for my sump pump?

It's worth considering if your home has experienced basement flooding before, or if storms in your area tend to come with power outages, since that's exactly when a standard pump can't help.

Can a sump pump be paired with basement waterproofing?

Yes, and it often should be. See our waterproofing the space page for how the two work together as a complete moisture management system.

Before We Arrive

What to do while you wait

Never stand in water near an active or failed sump pump

Standing water near an electrical pump and its wiring is a real electrocution risk; avoid entering the area until power is confirmed safe.

Check the discharge line isn't blocked or frozen

A blocked or frozen discharge line can cause the pump to run without actually removing water, giving a false sense that the system is working.

Don't wait for a storm to test your pump

Testing during dry weather, rather than discovering a failure mid-storm, is the single most effective preventive step a homeowner can take.

Have an emergency plan for pump failure during a storm

Knowing where portable pumps or wet-vacs are, and having our number on hand, matters more in the middle of an active storm than after.
