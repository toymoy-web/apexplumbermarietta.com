Local Conditions

The housing era behind Historic Marietta's plumbing calls

The original galvanized and early copper supply lines common in this historic core are several decades past typical service life. It shows up as reduced water pressure. In some homes, it shows up as visible rust in the water itself. The pier-and-beam and shallow masonry foundations typical of pre-1940s construction here also change plumbing access compared to a slab-on-grade home. That changes how a repair gets approached. Homes closer to Glover Park and the Square itself tend to be the oldest on our routes. Newer infill construction nearby has often already been repiped, and calls in far less often.

Warning Signs

Signs tied to Historic Marietta's building age

Low water pressure throughout the house

A classic sign of internal corrosion narrowing old galvanized pipe, common across the historic district's original plumbing.

Rust-colored water at the tap

Points to galvanized pipe corrosion rather than a water heater issue when it happens at multiple fixtures.

Slow-draining cast-iron waste lines

Original cast-iron drain lines from this era accumulate scale differently than modern PVC, and often need different clearing methods.

Standing water in yards after storms

Dense tree canopy around the Square holds ground moisture near older foundations longer than in newer, more open subdivisions.

A water heater original to a pre-1960s remodel

Older homes near the Square that haven't had their water heater replaced in a remodel are frequently running units well past typical service life.

Before We Arrive

If you have a burst pipe or active leak

Shut off the main valve

Stopping the water at the source matters more than anything else in the first few minutes.

Kill power to wet areas

If water is near an outlet or appliance, shut off power to that area at the breaker if it's safe to reach.

Clear the immediate area

Move furniture, rugs, and electronics away from the water while you wait, if it's safe to do so.

Local FAQ

Historic Marietta questions

Are galvanized pipes still common in Historic Marietta?

Yes, in a meaningful number of the pre-1940s homes around the Square that haven't been fully repiped. If your water pressure has dropped gradually over years, galvanized corrosion is the first thing we check.

Do you work around historic home foundations?

Yes. Pier-and-beam and shallow masonry foundations common in this district require a different access approach than a slab home, and our technicians work with that in mind rather than treating every house the same.

Is it worth repiping an older home in the historic district?

Often yes, if the home is still running original galvanized supply lines, since the corrosion inside those pipes only gets worse. We'll run tracking down a hidden leak and assess your specific system before recommending it.

Do you offer 24/7 emergency service in the historic district?

Yes, emergency dispatch covers the historic district the same as the rest of our Marietta coverage, including nights, weekends, and holidays.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Historic Marietta?

Yes. We cover every ZIP in the historic core, including 30060, 30064, 30006, 30008, from the Church Street-Cherokee Street Historic District around Marietta Square out through the Whitlock Avenue corridor.
