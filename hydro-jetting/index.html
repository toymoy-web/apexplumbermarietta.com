Warning Signs

Signs you need Hydro Jetting in Marietta

A clog that returns within weeks of snaking

Snaking clears a path through a clog without removing the buildup lining the pipe, which is why it often comes back.

Multiple slow drains throughout the house

Widespread scale or grease buildup narrowing several lines rather than a single isolated blockage.

Root intrusion confirmed by a camera inspection

Recurring grease clogs in a commercial setting usually need jetting rather than repeated snaking.

A commercial line with a documented grease-trap history

Recurring grease clogs downstream of a grease trap are a common candidate for scheduled jetting rather than reactive snaking.

Local Cause

Why this happens in Marietta

In Marietta's older neighborhoods, decades of grease buildup and root intrusion from mature trees narrow the interior of cast-iron and clay lines over time. Cable snaking punches a hole through a clog; hydro jetting scours the full interior diameter of the pipe clean. Commercial kitchens throughout the area see a similar pattern with grease specifically, where a trap that's overdue for cleaning lets buildup reach the line downstream of it.

Diagnosis

How we find the actual problem

For a recurring clog, we typically run a camera inspection first to confirm what's actually causing it, root intrusion, grease buildup, or scale, since that determines whether jetting alone will resolve it or whether a structural repair is also needed.

What This Means

"They Snaked It and It Got Stuck": When Snaking Isn't Enough

A common story we hear: a line was snaked, it worked briefly, then clogged again a few weeks later, or the snake itself got stuck partway through. That pattern usually means the underlying buildup, grease, scale, or root intrusion, was never actually removed, just pushed past. Hydro jetting addresses the buildup itself rather than punching through it.

Common Questions

Hydro Jetting FAQ

Is hydro jetting safe for old pipes?

We check the pipe's age, material, and condition first, often with a camera, and adjust pressure accordingly rather than running one setting for every job. A severely deteriorated pipe may need repair before jetting is appropriate.

How is hydro jetting different from snaking?

Snaking punches a path through a clog; hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to clear the full interior diameter of the pipe, which is why it tends to last longer for recurring clogs.

Can hydro jetting damage my pipes?

When pressure is matched to the pipe's actual condition, jetting is a standard, safe method. The risk comes from using excessive pressure on already-compromised pipe, which is exactly why we check condition first.

How often should a line be jetted preventively?

For a line with a history of grease or root issues, an annual preventive jetting is common; a line with no recurring problems doesn't need it on a fixed schedule.
