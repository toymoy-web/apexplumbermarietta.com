Warning Signs

Signs you need Water Heater Repair in Marietta

Inconsistent hot water

Water that starts hot and turns cold quickly often points to a failing heating element or a sizing mismatch for the household.

Pilot light or ignition issues

A gas water heater that won't stay lit usually points to a thermocouple or gas valve issue rather than the burner itself.

Leaking from a valve or fitting

Often a repairable fitting or valve issue rather than a sign the tank itself has failed.

Water heater leak near the tank warm spot

A localized leak at a connection point is a different repair than water pooling from the tank body itself, and the two get diagnosed differently.

Local Cause

Why this happens in Marietta

Most repair calls in Marietta fall into two categories: a component failure on a unit that's still within its useful life, like a thermostat, heating element, or pressure relief valve, or an early warning sign on an older tank that's approaching replacement age. Telling the two apart matters: repairing a component on a tank with years of life left is a straightforward fix, while the same symptom on a tank already past 10 years often means the repair only buys a short amount of time before the tank itself fails.

Diagnosis

How we find the actual problem

We isolate whether a symptom is coming from a specific component (thermostat, heating element, valve, thermocouple) or from the tank itself. Component failures on a tank still within its service life are usually straightforward repairs; leaks from the tank body itself point toward replacement instead. Water heater repairs and replacements follow the Georgia State Minimum Standard Plumbing Code (IPC-based) and the International Fuel Gas Code where a gas connection is involved.

Your Options

Repair options, honestly compared

Component replacementHeating elements, thermostats, thermocouples, and valves are repaired or replaced individually when the tank itself is sound, the same approach we use on tankless units when a specific part fails. Flush and descaleSediment buildup causing rumbling noises or reduced efficiency is cleared with a full tank flush. Referral to replacementWhen the tank itself has failed or is old enough that a repair isn't cost-effective, we'll say so directly rather than patching an aging unit.

Common Questions

Water Heater Repair FAQ

Is it cheaper to repair or replace my water heater?

It depends on the age of the unit and what's actually failed. A thermostat or element on a 5-year-old tank is worth repairing; a tank body leak on a 12-year-old unit usually isn't.

Why does my water heater smell like sulfur?

That's typically a reaction between the anode rod and sulfur bacteria in the water supply, not a safety issue on its own, but worth having checked.

Can a water heater leak cause water damage elsewhere in the house?

Yes, especially in a second-floor or attic installation. If the leak has already caused damage beyond the unit itself, that's a separate drying and restoring the space issue rather than a plumbing repair.

Do you carry common water heater parts on the truck?

Yes, the most frequently replaced components (thermostats, heating elements, thermocouples) are stocked on the truck, which is why most repairs complete in a single visit.

Before We Arrive

What to do while you wait

Turn off the water heater

For an electric unit, switch off the breaker; for gas, turn the gas control to "off" or "pilot." This stops the unit from heating an empty or leaking tank.

Shut off the cold water supply

The shutoff valve on top of most tank units stops more water from entering a tank that's already leaking.

Don't run hot water taps

Running hot water on a suspected failing tank can accelerate a leak or, on a gas unit with an ignition issue, isn't worth the risk until it's checked.

Call before you troubleshoot a gas smell

If you smell gas near the unit, leave the area and call your gas utility's emergency line before calling for a repair.
