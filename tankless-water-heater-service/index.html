Tankless Water Heater Service in Marietta, GA

Tankless water heaters need a different maintenance routine than a tank unit, and a household that skips it usually finds out the hard way when efficiency drops or error codes start appearing. We install, flush, descale, and repair tankless units for Marietta homes.

Warning Signs

Signs you need Tankless Water Heater Service in Marietta

Reduced hot water output

A tankless unit that used to run strong but now struggles is a classic scale-buildup symptom.

Error codes on the unit display

Modern tankless units display specific codes for flow, ignition, or temperature sensor issues rather than failing silently.

Longer wait for hot water

Can point to scale restricting flow or, separately, to distance from the unit, which is a plumbing layout issue rather than a unit problem.

Local Cause

Why this happens in Marietta

Cobb County's moderately hard water is the main driver of tankless service calls here. Mineral scale builds up inside the unit's heat exchanger faster than in a tank system, and manufacturers generally recommend an annual descaling flush in areas with this water hardness profile, which most homeowners never hear about at installation. A water softener reduces how fast that scale builds up in the first place.

Diagnosis

How we find the actual problem

We check the unit's error history and flow rate first, since most tankless problems in this market are scale-related rather than a component failure. A flush resolves the majority of reduced-output calls; a persistent issue after flushing points to a component that needs replacing.

What This Means

"It Needs a Service": What That Actually Means

Homeowners often call after being told by someone else that their unit "needs a service" or "it's been a few years" without more detail than that. In practice, that almost always means the heat exchanger needs a descaling flush, a maintenance step that isn't optional in an area with Cobb County's water hardness the way it might be in a soft-water region.

Your Options

Common Questions

Tankless Water Heater Service FAQ

How often does a tankless water heater need service?

An annual descaling flush is the standard recommendation for Cobb County's water hardness, even if the unit seems to be working fine.

Can I flush a tankless unit myself?

It's possible with the right isolation valves already installed, but most homeowners don't have those valves in place, which is itself something we can add during a service visit.

Why did my tankless unit throw an error code out of nowhere?

Scale buildup crosses a threshold gradually, so the unit can run fine for a long stretch and then throw a flow or temperature error once it finally restricts output enough to trigger the sensor.

Does a tankless unit need service even if it seems fine?

Yes. Scale buildup happens gradually and often doesn't show a symptom until it's already reduced output or tripped an error code, which is exactly why the annual flush is worth doing on schedule rather than waiting for a problem.
