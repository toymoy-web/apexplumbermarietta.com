Warning Signs

Signs you need Basement Waterproofing in Marietta

Visible water intrusion after heavy rain

The clearest sign that current waterproofing, if any exists, isn't handling the water load.

A persistent musty odor

Often a sign of chronic low-level moisture even without visible standing water.

Efflorescence (white, chalky deposits) on basement walls

A sign that water has been moving through the concrete or masonry over time.

A sump pump that runs constantly during wet weather

Can indicate the waterproofing system is working hard to keep up, worth evaluating whether additional measures are needed.

Cracks in the foundation wall itself

Structural cracks are a different, more serious issue than surface moisture and may need evaluation beyond waterproofing alone.

Local Cause

Why this happens in Marietta

Georgia's humid summers and occasional heavy rain events put real pressure on basement waterproofing, particularly in homes with older foundations or grading that directs water toward the house rather than away from it. Homes in Marietta's older, established neighborhoods with decades-old foundations tend to show these issues more often than newer construction with current grading and drainage standards built in from the start.

Diagnosis

How we find the actual problem

We assess where water is actually entering, through walls, the floor-wall joint, or from grading and drainage issues outside the foundation, since the entry point determines which waterproofing approach will actually work.

Common Questions

Basement Waterproofing FAQ

Is a sump pump enough, or do I need full waterproofing?

It depends on the severity and source. A sump pump manages water that's already entered; if intrusion is severe or chronic, addressing the entry point itself is usually the more durable fix.

Can waterproofing prevent mold in my basement?

Reducing moisture is the most effective way to prevent mold growth, though existing mold needs its own remediation separate from waterproofing the space going forward.

How do I know if my basement issue is a plumbing leak or groundwater?

A plumbing leak is usually localized near a pipe or fixture; groundwater intrusion typically shows up more broadly after rain. We can help distinguish the two during an assessment.

Will waterproofing increase my home's resale value?

A documented, professionally addressed moisture issue is generally viewed more favorably by buyers than visible water damage or an unresolved history of basement flooding.

How long does a waterproofing project typically take?

An interior drainage system is often completed in one to two days; exterior waterproofing takes longer depending on the extent of excavation required around the foundation.

Does insurance cover basement waterproofing?

Standard homeowners insurance typically doesn't cover preventive waterproofing, though damage from a sudden event may be covered separately. We can help document conditions either way.

Before We Arrive

What to do while you wait

Don't ignore a musty odor

A persistent musty smell often means mold is already developing even without visible water; earlier action limits how much remediation is eventually needed.

Avoid storing valuables directly on a basement floor

Even a minor, undiagnosed moisture issue can damage stored items over time; elevating storage is a simple precaution while a waterproofing plan is worked out.

Check window wells and exterior grading after storms

Standing water pooling against the foundation after rain is often a visible clue pointing to the entry source.

Address active mold before waterproofing work begins

If mold is already visible, that needs its own remediation; waterproofing prevents future moisture but doesn't resolve existing mold on its own.
