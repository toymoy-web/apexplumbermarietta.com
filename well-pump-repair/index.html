Well Pump Repair in Marietta, GA

A small number of Marietta-area properties, mostly on larger lots at the edges of our coverage area, rely on a private well rather than municipal water. We diagnose and repair residential well pump systems.

Warning Signs

Signs you need Well Pump Repair in Marietta

No water at all from the well system

Can indicate a failed pump, a tripped breaker, or a pressure switch issue depending on what else is affected.

Water pressure that cycles or pulses

Often points to a waterlogged pressure tank or a failing pressure switch.

The pump cycling on and off rapidly

Short-cycling like this puts extra wear on the pump motor and usually points to a pressure tank issue.

Sediment or cloudiness in well water

Can indicate pump or well condition issues, though this sometimes requires assessment beyond the pump itself.

Air sputtering from faucets along with the water

Can indicate the well is running low or the pump is drawing air, both worth investigating rather than ignoring.

Local Cause

Why this happens in Marietta

Well systems in this area are far less common than municipal connections, typically found on larger, more rural lots where municipal water service doesn't reach, and pump issues here often relate to age, electrical supply, or pressure tank problems rather than the well itself. A well that's served a household reliably for years can still develop a sudden pump or tank issue without any warning sign the system itself is failing.

Diagnosis

How we find the actual problem

We check the pressure tank, pressure switch, and electrical supply to the pump before assuming the pump itself has failed, since a pressure tank or switch issue is a more common and less costly cause of similar symptoms. Well system work follows the Georgia State Minimum Standard Plumbing Code and applicable well construction standards.

Common Questions

Well Pump Repair FAQ

How do I know if it's the pump or the pressure tank?

Pulsing or cycling pressure often points to the pressure tank; a complete loss of water more often points to the pump or its electrical supply. We check both rather than assuming.

How long do well pumps typically last?

Most submersible well pumps last 10 to 15 years with normal use, though water quality and how hard the pump has to work affect that range.

Do you also test well water quality?

We can flag obvious signs of a quality issue during a service call, and can also install water filtration if the results point that direction, but a full water quality test typically goes through a certified water testing lab rather than a plumbing service visit.

What's the difference between a jet pump and a submersible pump?

A jet pump sits above ground and pulls water up; a submersible pump sits inside the well itself. Which type your system has affects both diagnosis and repair approach.

Can a well run dry, and what happens if it does?

Yes, especially during extended dry periods. A well running dry shows up as reduced or no water output and, unaddressed, can damage the pump by having it run without adequate water to cool it.
