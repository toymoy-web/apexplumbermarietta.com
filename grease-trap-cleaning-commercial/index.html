Local Cause

Why this happens in Marietta

Restaurants and food-service businesses around Marietta are typically required to maintain grease traps or interceptors under local health and plumbing code, and a trap that's overdue for cleaning is a common cause of downstream clogs we're called out for separately under our Hydro Jetting service. Health inspectors specifically check maintenance records during routine visits, which is another reason to keep cleaning on a documented, regular schedule rather than reactively.

Diagnosis

How we find the actual problem

We assess the trap's current grease and solids level against capacity, and check whether the cleaning interval currently in place actually matches the kitchen's real volume, since a schedule set once at installation doesn't always account for a business's actual usage.

Common Questions

Grease Trap Cleaning (Commercial) FAQ

How often should a commercial grease trap be cleaned?

It depends on the kitchen's volume, but many commercial kitchens need cleaning every 1 to 3 months. We can help set a schedule matched to your actual usage rather than a generic default.

What happens if I skip grease trap cleaning?

Beyond potential health code violations, a neglected trap is a common cause of downstream sewer line clogs, which can mean a more disruptive and costly repair than routine cleaning would have been.

Do you handle grease trap cleaning for multiple locations?

Yes, we work with businesses that have more than one location and can coordinate a consistent cleaning schedule across all of them.

Can grease trap cleaning be scheduled outside business hours?

Yes, we regularly schedule grease trap service before or after operating hours to avoid disrupting kitchen operations.

Is grease trap waste hazardous material?

It's regulated waste requiring proper disposal through a licensed hauler, though it's not classified the same as hazardous chemical waste. We handle disposal in accordance with local regulations as part of the service.

Can a grease trap be too small for my kitchen?

Yes, an undersized trap for the kitchen's actual volume will need more frequent cleaning regardless of schedule, and upsizing is sometimes the more cost-effective long-term fix.

Before We Arrive

What to do while you wait

Ensure the area is ventilated before servicing

Decomposing grease and organic waste can produce gases that concentrate in an enclosed space; proper ventilation during cleaning matters for staff and technician safety.

Keep the trap area clear of stored items

Boxes or equipment stored over or near a grease trap slow emergency access and complicate routine cleaning.

Report any sewage odor to us promptly

An unusual odor beyond typical grease smell can indicate a trap that's overdue or a downstream issue worth checking sooner rather than later.

Never attempt to clean a trap without proper equipment

Grease trap waste requires specific handling and disposal; this isn't a task for general kitchen staff to take on themselves.
