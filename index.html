Why Marietta Calls Us

Built for Marietta's specific plumbing problems

Most Marietta homes were built between the 1970s and late 1990s. That means the original copper supply lines running through them are now 30 to 50 years old. That single fact drives most of what we see in the field. Pinhole leaks show up as a warm spot on the floor or a damp patch on a ceiling. Slab leaks raise a water bill before anyone notices a puddle. Water heaters get skipped past their replacement date. Cobb County adds a second layer: humid summers and the occasional hard winter freeze. Exposed pipe in crawl spaces and along exterior walls was never insulated for the cold snaps this area still gets. When a call comes in, we start by figuring out which pattern we're dealing with before we talk about a fix.

What counts as a plumbing emergency

Not every plumbing problem can wait for a weekday appointment. These are the calls we treat as same-hour priority, 24/7:

Burst or cracked pipe

Water actively flowing where it shouldn't. Shut off the main and call immediately.

Before We Arrive

What to do while you wait

A few steps in the first few minutes make a real difference to how much damage a leak or burst pipe causes before a technician gets there.

Shut off the water

Know where your main shutoff valve is before an emergency happens. Turning it off stops the source, not just the symptom.

Cut power near standing water

If water is pooling near outlets or a water heater, shut off power to that area at the breaker if it's safe to reach.

Move what you can

Get furniture, rugs, and electronics away from the water while you wait, if it's safe to do so.

Document before cleanup

A few photos of the standing water and its source are useful if you end up filing an insurance claim.

Older Homes vs. Newer Construction

Marietta's two very different plumbing profiles

Cobb County isn't one uniform market. Treating it that way leads to the wrong diagnosis. The east side of the county, including East Cobb and the Indian Hills area, was largely built out in the 1970s through 1990s on copper supply lines. Those lines are well past typical service life. Those homes are the ones we see for slab leaks, hidden pinhole leaks inside walls, and water heaters that finally give out after two or three decades of service. Newer construction on the west side of the county, and the growth around Kennesaw State University, runs PEX and modern copper instead. Those calls skew toward garbage disposals, faucet cartridges, and appliance connections rather than supply-line failures.

1970s-90s homes

Original copper approaching or past 30-50 years old. Slab leaks and pinhole leaks are the most common calls.

Newer construction

PEX and modern copper. Fixture, appliance, and garbage disposal calls are more typical than supply-line failures.
