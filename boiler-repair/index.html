Warning Signs

Signs you need Boiler Repair in Marietta

No heat or inconsistent heat output

Can point to a pressure issue, a failed circulator pump, or a thermostat problem depending on the system.

Unusual noises from the boiler

Kettling or banging sounds often indicate mineral buildup or trapped air in the system.

Visible leaking around the unit or piping

Worth addressing promptly, as boiler leaks can indicate pressure relief valve or seal issues.

A pilot light that won't stay lit

Similar to a gas water heater issue, often a thermocouple or gas valve problem.

Uneven heat between rooms or radiators

Can indicate trapped air or a circulation issue affecting how heat distributes through the system.

Local Cause

Why this happens in Marietta

Boiler systems are most often found in Marietta's older homes, particularly in the historic district and other pre-1970s construction, where radiant heat systems were more common than they are in newer subdivisions. A boiler original to a home from that era is now well past the point where component wear and mineral scale buildup are ordinary rather than exceptional.

Diagnosis

How we find the actual problem

We check system pressure, the circulator pump, and, for gas units, the ignition system to identify the specific point of failure before recommending a repair, since boiler issues often have several possible causes with a similar symptom.

Your Options

Repair options, honestly compared

Component repairCirculator pump, pressure relief valve, or ignition component repair for a system otherwise in good condition. System bleeding and pressure adjustmentResolving trapped air or incorrect pressure that's causing noise or uneven heating. Referral to replacementFor an older boiler with a significant failure, an honest assessment of whether repair or replacement makes more financial sense long term, the same judgment call we walk through on our repairing the tank itself page.

Common Questions

Boiler Repair FAQ

Are boilers still a good heating option?

They're less common in newer construction, but a well-maintained boiler in an older home can still provide reliable, even heat. Whether to keep or replace one is usually a case-by-case decision based on the system's current condition.

Why is my boiler making a banging noise?

Often called kettling, this is usually caused by mineral scale buildup on the heat exchanger or trapped air in the system, both addressable without full replacement in most cases.

How often does a boiler need maintenance?

Annual maintenance is a reasonable standard for most residential boilers, checking pressure, the circulator pump, and combustion components on gas units.

Can a boiler be converted to a different heating system?

It's possible but a significant project involving new equipment and ductwork or radiant lines; we can discuss whether that makes sense compared to maintaining the existing boiler.

Is it normal for a boiler to need water added periodically?

A well-sealed system shouldn't need frequent water added; if you're topping it off regularly, that usually points to a slow leak somewhere in the system worth locating rather than just continuing to add water.

Is boiler maintenance different from a furnace's?

Yes, boilers involve water and pressure components a furnace doesn't have, which means different maintenance points, circulator pumps, pressure relief valves, and expansion tanks specifically.

Before We Arrive

What to do while you wait

Never attempt to relieve boiler pressure yourself

The pressure relief valve is a safety component; if it's discharging or you suspect a pressure problem, shut down the system and call rather than adjusting it yourself.

Leave the area and call your gas utility if you smell gas

A gas smell near a boiler is a safety emergency independent of the boiler issue itself; treat it accordingly before addressing anything else.

Don't ignore a carbon monoxide detector alarm

Any working gas appliance, including a boiler, warrants a functioning CO detector nearby; an alarm should always be taken seriously and the area ventilated immediately.

Shut off the system if you notice active leaking

Water pooling near a boiler is worth shutting the system down for until it's assessed, both for safety and to limit water damage.
