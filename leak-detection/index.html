Warning Signs

Signs you need Leak Detection in Marietta

Unexplained rise in the water bill

The meter keeps running even with every fixture in the house off, a strong sign of a leak somewhere in the system.

Warm spot on the floor

Often a sign of a hot water line leaking under a slab, since the escaping hot water warms the surrounding concrete.

Sound of running water with everything off

A hissing or running sound with no fixture in use points to a leak in the supply line itself.

Damp patch or mold on a wall or ceiling

A slow leak inside a wall cavity often shows up as discoloration or a musty smell before any visible water appears.

Local Cause

Why this happens in Marietta

Given Marietta's housing stock, most hidden leaks trace back to the same root cause: original copper supply lines from the 1970s-90s construction era, now 30 to 50 years old and past typical service life. A leak under a slab foundation is especially common in this market because so much of the housing stock from that era was built slab-on-grade with copper lines running through the concrete.

Diagnosis

How we find the actual problem

We use acoustic leak detection equipment to listen for the sound of water escaping under pressure, combined with thermal imaging to spot temperature differences a hot water leak creates in a slab or wall. For a suspected slab leak specifically, this pinpoints the leak's location within a small area rather than requiring the whole slab to be opened. Any resulting repair follows the Georgia State Minimum Standard Plumbing Code, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC).

What This Means

"I Don't Know Where It's At": Finding a Leak You Can't See

Most calls for this service start the same way: the homeowner knows something is wrong, usually from the water bill or a warm spot on the floor, but has no idea where the leak actually is. That's exactly what electronic leak detection is for. Acoustic and thermal imaging equipment locates the leak's position without any exploratory demolition, so the repair, once we know where to look, is targeted instead of a guess.

Your Options

Repair options, honestly compared

Spot repairOnce the leak is located, a targeted repair at that specific point, the most common outcome for a single, isolated leak. RerouteRunning a new line around the damaged section instead of repairing in place, often the better option for a slab leak to avoid breaking concrete. Repipe evaluationIf the leak detection process reveals this is one of several leaks or the copper is broadly failing, our whole-home repiping service covers that next step.

Common Questions

Leak Detection FAQ

Do you have to cut into my slab to find a leak?

No. Electronic leak detection locates the leak first, using acoustic and thermal equipment, so any cutting that follows is targeted to the actual leak location rather than exploratory.

How much water can a hidden leak actually waste?

Even a small, slow leak can run continuously for weeks before it's noticed, which is why a sudden jump in the water bill with no change in household water use is worth investigating.

Can a leak detection find a leak in the yard, not just inside the house?

Yes, the same acoustic equipment traces the water service line from the meter to the house, which covers the yard portion of the line, not just what's under the slab or inside the walls.

Before We Arrive

What to do while you wait

Shut off the main water valve

If you suspect an active leak rather than a slow one, shutting off the main stops the water loss while you wait, even if you don't know the exact source yet.

Don't ignore a sudden water bill spike

A jump in usage with no change in household habits is worth investigating promptly. The longer a hidden leak runs, the more water and potential damage it causes.

Note when and where you notice symptoms

A warm spot's location, when a sound started, or which room shows dampness all help narrow down the search before equipment is even used.

Avoid DIY wall or slab cutting

Opening a wall or slab without first locating the leak electronically often misses the actual source and adds repair cost for no benefit.
