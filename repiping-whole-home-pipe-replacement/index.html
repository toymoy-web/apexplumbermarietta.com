Repiping & Whole-Home Pipe Replacement in Marietta, GA

When a home's supply lines are failing in more than one spot, patching each leak as it appears stops making financial sense. We repipe Marietta homes running on original 1970s-90s copper, replacing the whole system rather than chasing the next leak.

Warning Signs

Signs you need Repiping in Marietta

Repeated pinhole leaks

One leak is a repair; a second or third in different locations within a short window signals system-wide copper failure.

Low water pressure throughout the house

Internal corrosion narrowing pipe diameter across the whole system, not just a single blockage.

Discolored water from multiple fixtures

Corrosion inside old copper or galvanized pipe affecting the whole supply, not one isolated fitting.

Visible green or blue staining on fixtures

A classic sign of copper corrosion reacting with the water supply over decades.

Local Cause

Why this happens in Marietta

This is the network's core issue for Marietta: most of the housing stock was built in the 1970s through late 1990s, meaning the original copper supply lines are now 30 to 50 years old. A single pinhole leak is a repair. Multiple leaks appearing within a year or two of each other, especially in a home of that age, is a sign the whole system is nearing end of life.

Diagnosis

How we find the actual problem

We assess the age and material of the existing supply lines, check for signs of corrosion at accessible points, and review the leak history if there's already been more than one repair. For a home with confirmed original 1970s-90s copper and a pattern of recurring leaks, repiping is presented as an option alongside continuing to repair leak by leak, with the real cost trade-off explained for that specific situation. All repiping work is performed to the Georgia State Minimum Standard Plumbing Code, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC).

What This Means

When One Leak Means the Whole System

The hardest part of this decision for most homeowners is knowing when to stop repairing and start replacing. As a rough guide: a single leak in a home under 30 years old is usually just a repair. A second leak within a year or two, in a home already in the 30-50 year range typical of Marietta's housing stock, is the point where a full repipe usually costs less over the next five years than continuing to chase individual leaks.

Your Options

Repair options, honestly compared

Full copper repipeMatching the existing material for homeowners who prefer copper's longevity and are willing to pay the higher upfront cost. PEX repipeA lower-cost alternative to copper with strong resistance to the pinhole leaks that affect aging copper, increasingly the more common choice for a full-home repipe. Pricing

What affects the price

Every job is quoted after diagnosis and approved by you before work starts. These are the real factors that move the price up or down:

Copper vs. PEXCopper costs more upfront and lasts a comparable amount of time to what's already failing; PEX costs less and resists the pinhole-leak pattern common to aging copper.
Full vs. partial repipeA partial repipe limited to the failing section costs less than a whole-home repipe, but only makes sense if the rest of the system isn't showing the same age-related pattern.
Wall and floor accessHomes where pipes run through finished walls or slab take more time and patching work than homes with crawlspace or basement access.
Common Questions

Repiping FAQ

How long does a whole-home repipe take?

It varies by house size and whether walls need to be opened and patched, but most single-family repipes are completed within a few days rather than weeks.

Is PEX as durable as copper?

PEX doesn't develop the pinhole leaks that aging copper does, though it has its own considerations around UV exposure and rodent damage in exposed areas. We'll walk through the trade-offs for your specific home.

Do you have to cut into walls to repipe a house?

Some wall access is usually needed, though the amount varies by the home's layout and where the existing lines run. We patch and prep any opened areas as part of the job.
