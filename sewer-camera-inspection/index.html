Local Cause

Why this happens in Marietta

A camera inspection is especially useful in Marietta's older neighborhoods, where clay and early PVC sewer laterals installed decades ago can fail in several different ways, root intrusion, bellying, cracking, that each call for a different fix. Without a camera, it's genuinely difficult to tell these apart from the symptoms alone, which is why we recommend one before committing to a specific repair method rather than guessing. Newer PVC construction elsewhere in the county sees far fewer of these structural issues, so a camera inspection there is more often a confirmation than a diagnosis of an active problem.

Diagnosis

How we find the actual problem

A waterproof camera on a flexible cable is fed through the line, with a technician viewing the footage in real time to identify blockages, cracks, root intrusion, or structural issues, and recording the line's condition and, where useful, its depth and location for later reference. Any resulting repair recommendation is measured against the Georgia State Minimum Standard Plumbing Code, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC).

Your Options

Repair options, honestly compared

No action neededSometimes the honest finding is that the line is in acceptable condition and no repair is warranted yet. Targeted repair recommendationWhen damage is found, the camera footage points to the specific repair method that fits, spot repair, lining, or replacement. Documentation for real estate purposesFootage and a written summary for buyers, sellers, or agents who need documentation of a line's condition, and a recommendation for hydro jetting or repair if the camera finds an active issue.

Common Questions

Sewer Camera Inspection FAQ

Do I need a sewer camera inspection before buying a home?

It's a reasonable step for any home with an older sewer line, especially one with clay or early PVC piping, since a hidden structural issue can be an expensive surprise after closing.

Can a camera inspection find a leak, not just a clog?

Yes, the camera shows structural issues like cracks and root intrusion, not just blockages, which is part of why it's more informative than snaking alone.

How long does an inspection take?

Most single-family home inspections complete within an hour, depending on the line's length and how many cleanout access points are available.

Will the inspection damage my pipes?

No. The camera and cable are designed to pass through existing pipe without contact pressure, so the inspection itself doesn't add wear to the line.
