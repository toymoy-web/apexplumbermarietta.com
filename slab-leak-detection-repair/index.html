Warning Signs

Signs you need Slab Leak Detection in Marietta

A warm spot on the floor

A hot water line leaking under the slab warms the surrounding concrete, often the first sign a homeowner notices.

A sudden spike in the water bill

The meter keeps running even with every fixture off, a strong sign of a leak somewhere in the system.

The sound of running water with everything off

A hissing or running sound with no fixture in use often points to a slab leak specifically.

Cracks in flooring or baseboards

Water pressure building under a slab can, over time, cause visible cracking in tile or at baseboard trim.

Local Cause

Why this happens in Marietta

Slab leaks are the single most common serious plumbing issue we see in Marietta's 1970s-90s subdivisions, where copper supply lines were run directly through the concrete slab and are now 30 to 50 years old. That construction method, standard for the era, means a failing pipe leaks directly into or under the foundation rather than into an accessible crawlspace.

Diagnosis

How we find the actual problem

We use acoustic equipment to listen for the sound of water escaping under pressure and thermal imaging to spot the temperature difference a hot water leak creates in the slab. Together, these narrow the leak's location before any concrete is touched.

What This Means

Finding the Leak Without Breaking the Whole Slab

The instinct with a suspected slab leak is to assume the whole floor has to come up to find it. In practice, electronic leak detection narrows the location to a small area first, so any concrete that does get opened is targeted to that specific spot rather than a guess.

Your Options

Repair options, honestly compared

Spot repairOpening a small, targeted section of slab once the leak is precisely located, the most common repair for a single, isolated leak. RerouteRunning a new line above or around the slab instead of repairing in place, often preferred to avoid breaking concrete at all. Repipe evaluationIf this is one of several slab leaks in the same home, a full repipe is worth considering as the broader option.

Common Questions

Slab Leak Detection FAQ

How do I know if it's a slab leak and not just a foundation issue?

A slab leak usually comes with a water-related symptom, a rising bill, a warm spot, or the sound of running water, rather than the cracking-only pattern typical of foundation settling. We can help you tell the difference over the phone before scheduling a visit.

Is slab leak repair covered by homeowners insurance?

Often the water damage caused by a slab leak is covered, while the plumbing repair itself may or may not be, depending on your specific policy. We can provide documentation of what we find, but coverage questions go through your insurer.

Can a slab leak fix itself or get better on its own?

No. A slab leak gets worse over time as the surrounding concrete and soil continue to erode from the escaping water, which is why we treat a confirmed slab leak as something to address promptly rather than monitor.

Before We Arrive

What to do while you wait

Shut off the main water valve

If the leak is active rather than a slow seep, shutting off the main stops the water loss and limits further erosion of the concrete and soil around the leak while you wait.

Don't attempt to break into the slab yourself

Opening concrete without first locating the leak electronically often misses the actual source and can damage structural elements unnecessarily.

Note when symptoms started

A warm spot's exact location, when a water bill spike began, or when a sound was first noticed all help narrow down the leak before equipment is even used.

Watch for water reaching electrical outlets or systems

If water from the leak reaches an outlet or appliance, treat it as an electrical safety concern and shut off power to that area at the breaker if it's safe to do so.
