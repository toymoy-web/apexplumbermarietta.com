Sewer Line Repair in Marietta, GA

A damaged sewer line doesn't always announce itself as an emergency, but it rarely gets better on its own. We repair and replace damaged sewer lines for Marietta homes, from a localized crack to a fully collapsed section.

Warning Signs

Signs you need Sewer Line Repair in Marietta

Recurring sewer backups

A backup that clears with snaking but returns within weeks usually points to a structural problem, not a fresh clog.

Sewage odor in the yard or house

A cracked or broken sewer line can leak sewage into surrounding soil before it ever backs up into the house.

Unusually green or lush patches of lawn

A leaking sewer line acts as fertilizer for the grass directly above it, a real and recognizable sign.

Multiple drains backing up together

When more than one fixture backs up at the same time, the problem is usually in the main line, not an individual fixture.

Slow drainage that gradually worsens over months

A slowly narrowing or partially collapsed line often shows up as a gradual decline rather than a sudden backup.

Local Cause

Why this happens in Marietta

In Marietta's older neighborhoods, clay and early PVC sewer laterals installed decades ago are now old enough for root intrusion from mature trees to be a genuine, recurring cause of damage, on top of ordinary age-related deterioration. Newer PVC lines in the county's more recent construction hold up better against root intrusion, so the age and material of your specific line is usually the first thing worth confirming before assuming the worst.

Diagnosis

How we find the actual problem

A sewer camera inspection shows the actual condition of the line: root intrusion, a bellied section, a crack, or a fully collapsed run. That footage determines whether a spot repair, trenchless lining, or full replacement is the right call, rather than guessing from symptoms alone or from age and material assumptions. All repair work follows the Georgia State Minimum Standard Plumbing Code, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC).

Your Options

Repair options, honestly compared

Spot repairExcavating and replacing a single damaged section when the rest of the line is sound. Trenchless liningA structural liner installed inside the existing pipe, useful for root intrusion or cracking without needing to excavate the full run. For milder, earlier-stage root intrusion, hydro jetting alone sometimes resolves it first. Full replacementReserved for a line with damage along most of its length or a material (like old clay) that's failing broadly rather than at one point.

Pricing

What affects the price

Every job is quoted after diagnosis and approved by you before work starts. These are the real factors that move the price up or down:

Damage extentA single localized crack costs less to address than root intrusion or damage spanning a long section of line.
Repair methodSpot repair and trenchless lining generally cost less than full excavation and replacement.
Access and depthA sewer line under a driveway or mature landscaping costs more to access than one in open yard.
Common Questions

Sewer Line Repair FAQ

Do I need a permit to repair my sewer line?

Most sewer line repairs in Cobb County require a permit, which we pull on your behalf through the Cobb County Community Development Agency as part of the job.

Is trenchless repair always possible?

Not always. Trenchless lining works well for cracks and root intrusion in an otherwise structurally sound pipe, but a severely collapsed or misaligned section usually still needs excavation.

How long does a sewer line repair take?

A spot repair or trenchless lining is often completed in a single day; full replacement of a longer run can take several days depending on length and access.

Can a sewer line be repaired without excavating my whole yard?

In most cases, yes. A camera inspection first tells us exactly where the damage is, so spot repair or trenchless lining can target that specific section rather than the full run.
