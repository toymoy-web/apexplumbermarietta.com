Warning Signs

Signs you need Water Line Repair in Marietta

A wet spot in the yard between the meter and the house

Often the clearest visible sign of a service line leak.

Low water pressure throughout the house with a normal meter reading at the street

Points toward a problem in the line itself rather than the municipal supply.

A running water sound near the meter

The meter itself can sometimes be heard spinning even with all fixtures off.

Unusually green grass in a line along the service line's path

A leaking line acts as irrigation for the grass directly above it.

A drop in water pressure that started suddenly rather than gradually

A sudden pressure drop points more toward a new leak in the service line than gradual mineral buildup.

Local Cause

Why this happens in Marietta

In older parts of Marietta, original galvanized or early copper service lines are old enough that a leak between the meter and the house is a real, recurring issue, distinct from a leak inside the home's own plumbing system. Newer subdivisions with more recent service line materials see this far less often, so the age of your specific line is usually the first useful data point.

Diagnosis

How we find the actual problem

We use acoustic leak detection to trace the service line from the meter to the house and locate the leak's position before any digging starts, the same approach used for a hidden leak inside the home. Repairs follow the Georgia State Minimum Standard Plumbing Code, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC).

What This Means

"We Shut Everything Off and It Still Runs"

A common way this issue surfaces: a homeowner shuts off every fixture in the house, but the water meter keeps spinning. That points to a leak in the service line itself, between the meter and the house, rather than anything inside the home's own plumbing.

Your Options

Repair options, honestly compared

Spot repairRepairing a single, located leak in the line without replacing the full run. Full line replacementReserved for an older galvanized line with a leak history or advanced corrosion, where continued spot repairs aren't cost-effective. RerouteRunning a new line along a different path when the original route is difficult to access, the same option we consider for a hidden leak found through leak detection inside the home.

Common Questions

Water Line Repair FAQ

Am I responsible for the water line on my property?

Generally, yes. The utility typically owns and maintains the line up to the meter; the line from the meter to your house is the homeowner's responsibility in most Cobb County jurisdictions.

How do I know if the leak is my line or the utility's?

If the meter keeps running with every fixture in the house off, the leak is almost always on your side of the meter, which is the part you're responsible for.

Does the whole yard need to be dug up to fix a service line?

Not usually. Acoustic leak detection locates the leak first, so digging is typically limited to that specific section rather than the full line's path.

How long does a water line replacement take?

A straightforward residential service line replacement is often completed within a single day, depending on length, depth, and ground conditions.
