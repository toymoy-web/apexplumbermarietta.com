Local Cause

Why this happens in Marietta

Municipal water in the Cobb County system meets regulatory standards, but taste, chlorine smell, and mineral content still vary enough that a meaningful number of Marietta homeowners choose filtration for taste and comfort reasons rather than a documented health concern. Homes already running a water softener sometimes add filtration separately, since softening and filtration solve genuinely different problems even though they're often confused for the same thing.

Diagnosis

How we find the actual problem

We discuss what's actually motivating the interest in filtration, taste, specific contaminant concerns, or general water quality, since that determines whether a whole-home system, a point-of-use system, or both makes the most sense for the household.

What This Means

Whole-Home vs. Point-of-Use: Picking the Right System

The most common question we get isn't whether filtration is worth it, it's which type actually fits the goal. A whole-home system addresses every tap in the house, useful for chlorine smell or general water quality concerns. A point-of-use system at the kitchen sink is a lower-cost option when the only real goal is better-tasting drinking and cooking water. If hardness is the actual complaint rather than taste, a water softener is the better fit.

Common Questions

Water Filtration Installation FAQ

Is Marietta tap water safe to drink without filtration?

Municipal water in this area meets regulatory standards, so filtration here is generally about taste and comfort rather than a safety necessity. We can help you decide based on what's actually bothering you about your water.

Does a water softener also filter water?

No, a softener addresses hardness minerals specifically; filtration addresses taste, chlorine, and sediment. Some households benefit from both, installed together or separately depending on the goals.

How often do filter cartridges need replacing?

It depends on the system and household water usage, but most point-of-use cartridges need replacement every 3 to 6 months, with whole-home system filters on a longer cycle.

Will a whole-home filter reduce my water pressure?

A properly sized system shouldn't cause a noticeable pressure drop; we size the system to the home's actual flow demand specifically to avoid that trade-off.

Can filtration help with a specific health concern a doctor mentioned?

Certain systems target specific contaminants, but we'd recommend confirming the right filtration type against your actual water test results and your doctor's guidance rather than assuming any system addresses it.

Before We Arrive

What to do while you wait

A sudden change in water color or clarity

A sudden shift, rather than a gradual one, is worth having checked rather than masking with a filter alone; it can point to a municipal or plumbing-side issue beyond taste.

A strong or unusual odor from tap water

Chlorine smell is normal and addressed well by filtration; a sulfur, rotten-egg, or chemical odor is worth investigating rather than assuming filtration alone will resolve it.

Concerns after a boil-water notice

Filtration is not a substitute for following an active municipal boil-water advisory; continue following the utility's guidance until it's lifted.

Specific health concerns

If a doctor or specific test result raised a concern about your water, confirm the right filtration type against that actual result rather than assuming a general system addresses it.
