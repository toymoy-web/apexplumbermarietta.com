The Short Version

What actually differs between these areas

We get asked fairly often why we bother breaking Marietta into separate area pages instead of just listing one generic service area. The honest answer is that the housing stock genuinely differs enough to change what we expect to find on a given call.

Historic Marietta

The oldest housing stock in our coverage, much of it pre-1940s craftsman and bungalow construction around Marietta Square, still running original galvanized or early copper in a meaningful number of homes.

East Cobb & Indian Hills

Established 1960s-90s subdivisions where original copper supply lines are now 30 to 60 years old, the single biggest driver of the slab leak and pinhole leak calls we run across this entire service area.

West Cobb

Newer construction relative to the east side of the county, running PEX and modern copper, which shifts the typical call toward fixtures and appliances rather than aging supply lines.

Kennesaw

A genuine split city: an older downtown core with Marietta-style aging copper, and a substantial newer-construction footprint around Kennesaw State University.

How We Assign Technicians

Why local coverage actually matters here

Dispatching by area rather than treating Marietta as one undifferentiated zone is partly about response time and partly about diagnosis. A technician who works East Cobb regularly walks into a call already expecting aging copper and slab leak symptoms; the same technician working a West Cobb call that morning is thinking fixtures and appliance connections first. That local familiarity shortens the diagnostic step on almost every call, which is the main reason we organize coverage this way instead of rotating technicians randomly across the whole county.

It also means the questions we ask when you call differ by area. A call from Historic Marietta gets asked about the home's age and whether it's been repiped since original construction. A call from Kennesaw gets asked which side of town the address is on, since that alone tells us whether we're likely dealing with downtown-core aging copper or newer KSU-area construction. None of this changes our pricing or how fast we dispatch, it just means the technician who shows up already has a reasonable guess at what's wrong before touching anything.

This same logic is why the area pages exist at all rather than one generic "service area" page repeated with different names swapped in. Each page reflects the real housing-age and construction data for that specific place, not a template filled in from a spreadsheet. If you're comparing our East Cobb page to our West Cobb page, for instance, you'll notice the warning signs, the top services, and even the local FAQ are genuinely different, because the underlying plumbing realities in those two areas are genuinely different. That's also why we'd rather tell you upfront that a given area isn't a great fit for a specific service than force a page to exist for search purposes alone.

If you're a landlord or property manager with addresses spread across more than one of these areas, this same distinction is worth keeping in mind when you're deciding how to budget for maintenance. A portfolio weighted toward East Cobb or Indian Hills is statistically more likely to need leak detection and eventual repiping work than one weighted toward West Cobb's newer construction, and knowing that ahead of time tends to make for less surprising repair calls down the line.
