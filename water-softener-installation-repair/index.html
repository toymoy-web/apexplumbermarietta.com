Cobb County's moderately hard water takes a toll on pipes, water heaters, and fixtures over time. We install and repair whole-home water softener systems for Marietta homes looking to reduce that wear, sized to actual household usage rather than a generic default unit.

Local Cause

Why this happens in Marietta

The same moderate water hardness that accelerates scale buildup in Marietta's aging copper pipes and tankless water heaters also shows up as spotting on fixtures, reduced soap lather, and mineral buildup in appliances that use hot water. It's not extreme hardness by national standards, which is part of why a softener is a genuine trade-off decision here rather than an automatic recommendation on every job. Households already dealing with tankless descaling on a regular schedule, or a water heater showing early sediment buildup, tend to see the clearest benefit from adding a softener.

Diagnosis

How we find the actual problem

For a new installation, we size the system to the household's water usage and the water hardness level, checked against Cobb County's typical supply hardness rather than assumed. For a repair call, we check the resin tank, brine tank, and control valve to identify whether the issue is a regeneration cycle problem, a valve failure, or resin that needs replacing, since those three failure points call for genuinely different fixes.

Common Questions

Water Softener Installation FAQ

Is a water softener worth it in this area?

Cobb County's water hardness is moderate rather than extreme, so it's a genuine trade-off rather than a must-have. It tends to make more sense for households that have already noticed appliance wear or fixture spotting.

How often does a water softener need maintenance?

Most systems need periodic salt refills and occasional resin bed cleaning, with the exact schedule depending on household water usage and hardness level.

Can a water softener help my water heater last longer?

Reduced scale buildup from softened water can extend a tank water heater's service life, since sediment accumulation is a real factor in tank failure over time.

Does a water softener affect drinking water taste?

Some households notice a slightly different taste from softened water, since the process exchanges calcium and magnesium for sodium; a separate drinking water tap or filter is an option if that's a concern.
