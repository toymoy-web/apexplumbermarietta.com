Tankless Water Heater Maintenance: What Cobb County Homeowners Need to Know

Tankless units need a maintenance step most homeowners never hear about at installation, and skipping it is the main reason performance drops over time.

6 min readMarietta, GA

Tankless water heaters have a real advantage over tank units: no standing tank of water to run out, and a longer expected service life to go with it. But they also come with a maintenance requirement that tank units don't have, and it's one that installers don't always explain clearly at the time of purchase. If you own a tankless unit in the Marietta area, this is the single most important thing to know about keeping it running well.

The Core Issue

Cobb County's water hardness and your heat exchanger

Cobb County's municipal water supply is moderately hard, not extreme by national standards, but enough to matter for a tankless unit specifically. Inside the unit, water passes through a heat exchanger at high speed, and mineral content in the water gradually builds up scale on the interior surfaces of that exchanger. Over time, that scale buildup restricts flow and reduces the unit's ability to transfer heat efficiently.

This is different from a tank water heater, where sediment settles at the bottom of a large tank and takes much longer to become a real problem. A tankless unit's heat exchanger is a much smaller, more sensitive space, which is exactly why manufacturers generally recommend an annual descaling flush for units operating in areas with hardness levels like ours.

Signs You're Overdue

What reduced performance actually looks like

Reduced hot water output

A unit that used to run strong but now seems to struggle, especially with more than one fixture running.

Error codes on the display

Modern units show specific codes for flow or temperature sensor issues once scale restricts flow enough.

Longer wait times for hot water

Can point to scale, though it's worth ruling out simple distance from the unit as a separate cause first.

It's simply been over a year since the last flush

Scale builds up gradually, so a unit can seem fine right up until it crosses a threshold.

What a Descaling Flush Actually Involves

Not as invasive as it sounds

A descaling flush circulates a manufacturer-approved descaling solution, often a vinegar-based solution, through the heat exchanger for a set period, dissolving accumulated mineral scale without needing to disassemble the unit. The process is much easier when a unit already has isolation valves installed, which allow the flush to happen without disconnecting the unit from your home's plumbing. If your unit doesn't have these valves, adding them during a service visit is a one-time step that makes every future flush faster and less involved.

Beyond the Annual Flush

Other maintenance worth knowing about

Flushing addresses the most common issue, but a tankless unit can also develop problems with its ignition system, flow sensor, or temperature sensor independent of scale buildup. If a flush doesn't resolve reduced performance, that's usually the next thing worth checking, and it's a genuinely different repair than routine maintenance.

The bottom line for Marietta-area tankless owners: don't wait for a problem to show up before scheduling a flush. An annual maintenance visit is a small, predictable cost compared to premature failure, the same logic that applies to catching a tank water heater repair early rather than late.

Setting a Schedule

How to actually stay on top of it

The easiest way to avoid falling behind on tankless maintenance is to tie it to something you'll already remember, like the same month each year you handle other seasonal home maintenance, changing HVAC filters or checking smoke detector batteries. Some manufacturers include a maintenance reminder feature on newer units, but plenty of installed units don't, which means the schedule is genuinely on the homeowner to track rather than the unit itself prompting you.

If you're not sure when your unit was last flushed, or whether it ever has been since installation, that's common and not something to feel behind on. A first flush on a unit that's gone several years without one simply takes a bit longer to fully clear accumulated scale, but it's still a straightforward service call rather than a sign of damage that's already occurred.

Comparing to a Tank System

Is the maintenance requirement a real downside?

It's fair to ask whether this maintenance requirement offsets some of tankless's advantages. In practice, an annual flush is a relatively small, predictable cost compared to the ongoing sediment buildup that eventually shortens a tank water heater's life regardless of maintenance. The difference is that tankless maintenance is proactive and scheduled, while tank water heater failure is often a surprise. Homeowners who stay on schedule with flushing generally get the full expected service life out of a tankless unit, which tends to run longer than a comparable tank system.

If you're installing a tankless unit for the first time and haven't yet decided on a brand or model, it's worth asking your installer directly about the manufacturer's specific recommendation for your water hardness level, since some units are more tolerant of scale buildup than others by design. That single question at installation time can save some maintenance frequency down the road, though none of the major manufacturers eliminate the need for periodic flushing entirely in areas with hardness like ours.

A quick way to tell if you're due: check your installation paperwork or ask us during your next visit for any reason. We can check the unit's service history in most cases and let you know honestly whether a flush is overdue.
