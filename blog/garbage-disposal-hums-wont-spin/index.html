Why Your Garbage Disposal Hums But Won't Spin (And What to Do)

It's one of the most common disposal complaints, and it's usually a much simpler fix than homeowners assume.

4 min readMarietta, GA

A garbage disposal that hums when you flip the switch but doesn't actually spin is one of the most common calls we get, and the good news is that it's often a quick, inexpensive fix rather than a sign the unit has failed.

What's Actually Happening

A jammed impeller, not a dead motor

That humming sound means the motor is receiving power and trying to turn, but something is physically blocking the impeller from spinning. This is different from a unit that makes no sound at all, which usually points to an electrical issue instead. A hum with no spin is almost always a mechanical jam, something got caught in the grinding chamber that shouldn't have, a fruit pit, a piece of bone, or fibrous material like celery strings.

What You Can Try First

A safe, simple check before calling

Most disposals have a hex-shaped hole on the bottom of the unit designed for exactly this situation. Turning off power to the disposal at the switch or breaker first, you can insert an appropriately sized hex wrench into that hole and manually rotate it back and forth to free the jam. Many manufacturers also include a small reset button on the bottom of the unit that trips when the motor overheats trying to push through a jam, worth pressing after clearing the obstruction. Any professional repair or installation follows the Georgia State Minimum Standard Plumbing Code, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC).

When to Call Instead of DIY

Signs it's more than a simple jam

The hex wrench trick doesn't free it

Can indicate the jam is more severe or the impeller itself has an issue beyond a simple obstruction.

The unit leaks after you clear the jam

A separate issue, usually a failed seal, that a cleared jam won't resolve on its own.

It hums even with nothing visibly stuck

Worth a professional look, since something could be caught in a spot not visible from above or below.

You're not comfortable working near the disposal's wiring

There's no shame in preferring a professional handle it, especially near an electrical connection.

Preventing the Next Jam

What shouldn't go down a disposal

Fibrous vegetables like celery and corn husks, grease, coffee grounds in large quantities, and bones are the most common causes of jams. None of these need to be avoided entirely, but running cold water while the disposal operates meaningfully reduces how often you'll need disposal repair or a drain cleaning visit for the line downstream of it.

If It's Never Happened Before

A first-time jam versus a recurring pattern

A single jam, cleared with the hex wrench trick, isn't cause for concern about the unit's overall condition. But if you find yourself clearing a jam every few weeks on the same unit, that's a different situation. Recurring jams can indicate the disposal is undersized for how the household actually uses it, or that specific habits, like regularly disposing of fibrous scraps, are creating a repeating pattern worth addressing rather than continuing to clear the same jam repeatedly.

Age Matters Too

When a jam is really an early sign of motor wear

On an older unit, what looks like a simple jam can sometimes be a motor that's struggling generally, with the jam simply being the point where it finally stalls. If your disposal is approaching or past the typical 8 to 12 year service life and jamming has become more frequent than it used to be, that's worth mentioning during a service call rather than treating each jam as an isolated event.

A Word on Warranty

Check before you pay for a repair

If your disposal is relatively new, it's worth checking whether it's still under manufacturer warranty before paying for a repair visit, since some warranty terms cover motor issues for several years from installation. We're happy to help you determine whether a specific issue is likely warranty-covered before proceeding, rather than charging for a repair you might not need to pay for out of pocket.

One Last Tip

Running water matters more than people think

A surprising number of jams happen because the disposal was run without adequate water flow. Cold water helps solidify grease so it grinds rather than gums up the mechanism, and running it for a few seconds before and after grinding, not just during, gives the unit the best chance of clearing waste completely rather than leaving residue that contributes to the next jam.

Replacement Timing

When repair stops making sense

If a disposal is jamming frequently, is more than a decade old, and has already had one motor-related repair, replacement is usually the more sensible next step rather than another repair on a unit that's clearly nearing the end of its useful life. Newer units also tend to run quieter and grind more efficiently, which is worth factoring in if you're on the fence about replacing versus repairing an aging unit yet again.

Whatever the outcome, a humming disposal is rarely an emergency in the way a leak or a burst pipe is. It's worth addressing promptly for convenience, but you don't need to treat it as urgent in the way you would an active water issue.

Same-day or next-day scheduling is typical for a disposal issue that isn't causing active flooding, giving you time to decide whether to try the hex wrench trick yourself first.
